What a complete bid must include.

Run any builder's bid through this list before you sign. Written by a working custom-home builder. If a line fails the test, it's not a dealbreaker — it's a question to ask, in writing, before your leverage is gone.

Part 1 · Scopes that go missing

Is every one of these actually in the bid?

  • Site work & final grading — clearing, dirt work, and the final grade around the house
  • Driveway — with a length and a surface named, not a token "site allowance"
  • Utility connections — water/sewer taps or well & septic, power service, and who pays the fees
  • Permits & impact fees — they vary wildly by county and are rarely small
  • Gutters & downspouts — tiny line, chronically absent
  • Low-voltage: security, cameras, network, TV — if the plans show it, the bid must carry it
  • Appliances — and who supplies vs. who installs
  • Landscaping, sod & irrigation — usually excluded; fine — as long as it's excluded in writing
  • Window coverings, mirrors, shelving — the "we thought that was included" classics
  • Construction cleanup & final clean — someone pays for the dumpsters
Part 2 · The allowance floor test

Would each allowance actually buy what you've been picturing?

An allowance isn't a price — it's a placeholder. Set low, it makes the bid look cheap and guarantees a change order later. For every allowance, ask: "walk me through what this number buys on my layout."

  • Countertops — measured off your actual cabinet layout, not a round number
  • Cabinets — brand/line named, or it's a guess
  • Flooring — by area and material — a $12 tile and a $40 tile read the same on a plan
  • Lighting & plumbing fixtures — count the fixtures on your plan, divide the allowance, gut-check it
  • Front door / garage doors / hardware — small lines that swing thousands
Part 3 · Vague lump sums

Can every big number be broken down on request?

"Electrical — $35,000" with no detail isn't necessarily wrong. But it's unverifiable, and unverifiable lines are where change orders breed. A builder who can't break a number down didn't build the number up.

If it's not written, it's not included.
The single most expensive sentence in home building — learn it before you sign, not after.
Part 4 · The contract lines

Before you sign anything, find these in the contract:

  • Change-order pricing, defined — cost plus a fixed markup, in writing — not "re-quoted as they come"
  • A milestone payment schedule — pay for work completed, never far ahead of it
  • Allowance reconciliation — if you spend under an allowance, the difference comes back to you
  • Exclusions listed explicitly — a good builder is proud to list them; a thin bid hides them
  • Warranty terms and a dispute process — boring today, priceless in year two

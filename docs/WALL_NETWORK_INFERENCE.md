# Wall network interpretation

New-plan extraction and the live wall-run analysis can now infer wall-body candidates without individual wall decisions. The method uses unchanged native paired faces, a 3.5- or 5.5-inch depth matched to the frozen company assumption, perpendicular endpoint contacts and collinear fragments. It does not establish framing size from the drawing or certify wall assemblies.

The search requires two wall pieces at least four feet long with perpendicular contacts at both ends, in a connected group spanning at least six feet along each drawing axis. Collinear fragments can participate across gaps up to eight feet; those gaps remain unmeasured and are never added to wall length. Nearby parallel layers overlapping at least two feet within a one-foot separation are held out together unless exactly one unchanged outline physically meets independent perpendicular wall faces at both end caps. The competing outlines stay unresolved. These are recognition heuristics, not building-code requirements.

Parallel groups with edited members or explicit classifications are not resolved by this automatic rule. Both end caps need actual geometric contact; proximity alone is insufficient, and two supported alternatives remain ambiguous. Resolving a local outline does not remove the minimum network-anchor and extent requirements. Its evidence includes the unresolved competing source hashes, so changes to those outlines invalidate dependent interpretation. Current verification is described in [parallel wall layers](PARALLEL_WALL_LAYERS.md).

Explicit wall, nonwall and uncertain decisions retain precedence. Stale decisions are not replaced with automatic inference. Edited pieces cannot support the original network interpretation; their original paired source can still flag nearby layer conflicts. Unsupported depths, missing CAD-face evidence and disconnected fragments remain unresolved. Network hashes include supporting source geometry so downstream opening bindings change when their evidence changes.

Short rectangular outlines now participate when exactly one pair of native face directions agrees with the frozen stud-depth assumption. Both possible axes are checked; an equal-sided outline is not assigned a direction by its aspect ratio. Conflicting face directions, changed polygons and isolated pieces stay unresolved. The inferred axis is used for run analysis while the original four-point polygon remains editable and unchanged in the measurement source. No gap or short outline becomes a floor-area or lumber purchase quantity through this interpretation.

Inferred decisions carry `inferred: true` and `requires_review: true`. Runs name their inferred sources. No reviewer identity is invented; whole-wall purchase quantity and certification remain unavailable. The initial summary exposes inferred and unresolved counts, and the live API recomputes after measurement edits.

The earlier Burns short-direction development run started with a fresh intake using company profile version 13. Of 97 wall candidates, 93 were inferred, including all ten short outlines. Four possible layers remained unresolved. All 33 printed opening tags had location candidates. The 93 wall interpretations and ten short-piece axes agreed with the prior development review; both tub/laundry ambiguity pairs stayed unresolved. At that checkpoint, door roles remained unassigned. Later symbol and face-contact rules improve those counts as documented in the current verification. This comparison is not held-out or independent accuracy validation.

That checkpoint's verification included 131 targeted tests, fresh intake and real extraction without per-wall answers, an actual HTTP short-polygon edit/restoration and an explicit uncertain override. A separate live dependency check proved that editing the short outline removes its dependent opening location while retaining the printed tag as unresolved; restoration recovers all 33 locations. The original source archive and Roberts estimate snapshot were preserved. Verifiers are `work/phase3/verify_wall_network_inference.py` and `work/phase3/verify_short_wall_opening_dependencies.py`.

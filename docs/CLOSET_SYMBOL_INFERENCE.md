# Reach-in closet interpretation

The room pipeline can infer an unnamed reach-in closet from a native closed shelf rectangle with two rod lines near its front, spanning a narrow rectangular room opposite its sole interior opening. This is a disclosed estimating interpretation, not an approved finish selection or shelving purchase quantity.

The supported room is 20–40 inches deep and 36–120 inches along the shelf. The shelf front is 10–16 inches from the rear boundary; end/rear setbacks are at most 1.25 inches. Two distinct inner lines must sit 0.5–3 inches behind the front and 0.5–1.75 inches apart. Rectangle joints and rod endpoints must align within 0.01 inch. These limits describe this matcher, not construction requirements. White, invisible, dashed, incomplete, competing, nonrectangular, holed, externally connected, multiply connected and source-uncertain cases are withheld. Existing room decisions and printed labels retain precedence.

All paths, drawing indexes, plan hash, room binding and opening connection are retained. The sole entry into that reach-in closet supplies its served-room class. Ordinary doors inherit the saved core and hardware practice. Sliding pairs between supported interior regions remain one special assembly with two drawn panels; ordinary knob quantities and special-assembly core specifications are not invented.

The Burns development case produces four additional closet interpretations: two ordinary swinging doors and two special sliding pairs. It still uses reviewed sheet, scale and source-style setup. It is not an unseen-plan accuracy test. Measurements and interpretations remain reviewable, and there is no final house total or purchase release.
